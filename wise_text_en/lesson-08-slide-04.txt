Turning the Case into Audit Evidence

At this point, the audit work becomes a trace, not a debate. Maria starts from the intended-use statement. What does Meridian say the system is for? What use is prohibited? Do marketing materials say the same thing as the formal product file? If a sales deck says automatic diagnosis while the technical file says auxiliary prompt, that inconsistency is not a medical opinion. It is audit evidence about communication and responsibility boundary.

Then she moves to the risk assessment. Are missed detection, false alarm, physician over-reliance, data quality, image-device differences, model update effects, customer configuration, patient privacy and abnormal events actually identified? Are causes and existing controls recorded, or does the table only say model accuracy risk? Are residual risks accepted by a defined role with enough authority? If a high-caution system has residual risks but no recorded management decision, the assessment is not yet carrying the management system.

From there, Maria reads the impact assessment again. It should identify physicians, patients, hospital customers and Meridian as affected parties. It should describe how impact may be direct or indirect. It should evaluate severity, reversibility and the ability to correct or escalate a problem. It should say when the assessment is reviewed: before release, at major model updates, when customer deployment changes, when complaints show a pattern, when abnormal monitoring indicators appear, or when applicable obligations change.

She also looks for the bridge from assessment to evidence. If the risk is missed detection on images from older portable machines, the file should not stop at the word validation. It should show whether the validation plan included those devices, which data were used, who was competent to judge the test set, what limitations were recorded, how the release decision handled remaining uncertainty, and what customer instructions were updated. This is where audit evidence becomes more than a folder name. It becomes a chain of decisions that can be followed.
