Case Setup: The Comfortable but Dangerous Answer

Let us make the lesson concrete. The organization is MetroCare Health Network, working in multi-site healthcare certification. The sampled system is CareRoute AI Portfolio. MetroCare seeks certification for an AIMS covering multiple hospitals, an appointment triage tool, a radiology assistant and a generative administrative assistant.

The lead auditor is Grace Patel. Instead of beginning with a full-document request, Grace asks the auditee to open one hospital pilot of an appointment triage assistant missing from the central AI inventory. That choice matters because it gives the audit a real anchor before people start explaining the management system in general terms.

Then comes the comfortable but dangerous answer: Stage 1 only checks documents, so we do not need a system sampling strategy yet. It sounds efficient and reassuring. It also hides the weakness that the lesson is designed to expose.

The hidden issue is this: Without a credible inventory, scope boundary, obligation view and competence plan, Stage 2 sampling may miss the systems that define the AIMS risk profile. Once CareRoute AI Portfolio makes that issue visible, the audit stops floating above the organization. It asks whether MetroCare Health Network's governance reaches this use, this decision and this evidence trail.

Grace maps the roles before asking for more files. In the route, the auditor has to understand AIMS manager; hospital operations director; medical technology lead; certification planner; technical expert. The point is not to make the chart impressive; it is to know who can explain, approve, challenge or correct the AI-related decision.

The evidence objects are equally practical: audit programme; Stage 1 readiness notes; AI inventory; multi-site sampling plan; technical expert allocation record. For CareRoute AI Portfolio, none of these records is magic by itself. Their value appears when they fit together and explain what happened in the sample.

In MetroCare Health Network, comfort and evidence pull in different directions. The comfortable answer lowers the pressure in the room. Evidence for CareRoute AI Portfolio restores the useful pressure by asking where the answer operates, who relies on it and which record would reveal a break.

By the end of this case, the teaching point is not that MetroCare Health Network is careless. The point is more useful: CareRoute AI Portfolio can make a plausible management answer too thin when its real use creates a sharper evidence route.
