Audit Forward and Audit Backward

This is a good place to introduce two movements that make an AI audit much more practical: audit forward and audit backward. Audit forward starts from a planned change. Suppose Meridian releases version 3.2. The auditor follows the change request to the updated data source, validation plan, test coverage, device coverage, approval record, customer notification, training update and post-release monitoring plan. If the model changed but the impact assessment was not touched, the organization may have treated AI change as a technical event rather than an AIMS event.

Audit backward starts from something that happened in operation. Suppose a hospital reports that the system did not highlight an area later judged clinically important. The auditor does not decide whether the clinical judgement is correct. Instead, the auditor follows the internal route. Was the feedback logged? Was it classified for seriousness? Did technical, medical affairs and customer support personnel review it? Did it trigger retesting, customer communication, risk reassessment or impact-assessment review? Did management see recurring issues in monitoring or management review?

These two movements reveal the difference between maintenance and effectiveness. A maintenance-style audit asks whether the risk register, impact assessment and validation report exist. An effectiveness-style audit asks whether those records influence release decisions, customer instructions, oversight design, monitoring, corrective action and improvement. ISO/IEC 42001 does not reward paper that stays separate from decisions. It expects a management system that can learn from AI use.

The interview route should be just as connected. Maria does not ask every person the same question. With the product owner, she asks how intended use was defined and approved. With medical affairs, she asks how professional validation limits were set. With the data lead, she asks what device and institution differences were considered. With customer training, she asks what doctors are told at deployment. With technical support, she asks how feedback becomes evidence for review. The answers may differ in wording, but they should describe one coherent management system.
