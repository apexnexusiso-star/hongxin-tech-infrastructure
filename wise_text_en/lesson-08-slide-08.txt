Professional Boundary

One final boundary is essential. The auditor does not determine whether ClearView is medically valid, whether a specific image was diagnosed correctly, or whether a regulator should approve the device. Those judgements belong to qualified medical, technical and regulatory channels. The auditor's job is different and still very important. The auditor evaluates whether Meridian has identified applicable obligations, defined intended use, assessed AI risks and human impacts, performed suitable verification and validation through competent people, communicated limitations, monitored operation, handled feedback and used review triggers.

When you keep that boundary, your audit conclusion becomes stronger, not weaker. Instead of saying the model is unsafe, you may say the organization did not include patient impact and physician over-reliance in the impact assessment for a high-caution medical assistive system. Instead of saying the software is clinically wrong, you may say that customer feedback on missed prompts did not trigger documented review of risk, validation coverage or impact assessment. That is management-system language, grounded in evidence.

Practical Task and Reading Guidance

Here is your short practice task. Choose one AI system that you know, either from your organization or from a training case. Draw three columns. In the first column, write what may go wrong. In the second, write who may be affected and through what path. In the third, write the evidence an auditor could trace. Do not start with a checklist. Start with use. If the AI output changes a decision, a priority, a recommendation, a warning, a score or a service path, there is probably an impact path to understand.

A good answer does not need to be long. It needs to be connected. For a recruitment screening tool, the first column may say biased ranking. The second may say candidates from a certain background may lose interview opportunities without knowing how the score influenced the process. The third may say supplier evaluation, local validation, recruiter override records, candidate notice, appeal handling and monitoring of selection patterns. That is the same logic we used in the medical case, but applied to another use scenario without copying the story.

After this lesson, read textbook sections 5.1, 5.2 and 5.3 again, then read the medical assisted-diagnosis case in section 12.3. Pay special attention to the sentence that says impact assessment should consider physicians, patients, hospital customers and the enterprise's own responsibility. That one sentence is a useful test of whether a medical AI audit is still trapped inside model accuracy, or whether it has reached the management-system question.
