Case Setup: The Comfortable but Dangerous Answer

Let us make the lesson concrete. The organization is Falcon Components, working in manufacturing visual inspection. The sampled system is VisionLine QC. Falcon uses a vision AI system to identify defects on a high-speed production line. A customer order surge pressures managers to reduce manual reinspection.

The lead auditor is Hannah Cho. Instead of beginning with a full-document request, Hannah asks the auditee to open one production batch where reinspection was skipped after the system reported green performance indicators. That choice matters because it gives the audit a real anchor before people start explaining the management system in general terms.

Then comes the comfortable but dangerous answer: The AI policy is signed, and the Chief Digital Officer owns AI, so leadership responsibility is clear. It sounds efficient and reassuring. It also hides the weakness that the lesson is designed to expose.

The hidden issue is this: A named executive and signed policy do not prove that business managers, production owners and quality leaders follow governance when delivery pressure conflicts with AI controls. Once VisionLine QC makes that issue visible, the audit stops floating above the organization. It asks whether Falcon Components's governance reaches this use, this decision and this evidence trail.

Hannah maps the roles before asking for more files. In the route, the auditor has to understand plant manager; quality manager; CDO delegate; line supervisor; customer account lead. The point is not to make the chart impressive; it is to know who can explain, approve, challenge or correct the AI-related decision.

The evidence objects are equally practical: AI policy; responsibility matrix; production deviation record; governance committee minutes; management review input. For VisionLine QC, none of these records is magic by itself. Their value appears when they fit together and explain what happened in the sample.

In Falcon Components, comfort and evidence pull in different directions. The comfortable answer lowers the pressure in the room. Evidence for VisionLine QC restores the useful pressure by asking where the answer operates, who relies on it and which record would reveal a break.

By the end of this case, the teaching point is not that Falcon Components is careless. The point is more useful: VisionLine QC can make a plausible management answer too thin when its real use creates a sharper evidence route.
