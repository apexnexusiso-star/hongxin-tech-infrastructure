Case Setup: The Comfortable but Dangerous Answer

Let us make the lesson concrete. The organization is HelioBank, working in retail banking loan pre-assessment. The sampled system is PreScore Credit Navigator. HelioBank uses a vendor model inside its online loan journey. The bank configures eligibility thresholds, decides which products are offered and sends applicants next-step messages.

The lead auditor is Jonas Reed. Instead of beginning with a full-document request, Jonas asks the auditee to open a rejected small-business loan pre-assessment where the applicant received an automated next-step message. That choice matters because it gives the audit a real anchor before people start explaining the management system in general terms.

Then comes the comfortable but dangerous answer: The model is provided by the supplier, so we are not the provider and the AI responsibility stays mainly with them. It sounds efficient and reassuring. It also hides the weakness that the lesson is designed to expose.

The hidden issue is this: Supplier provision does not remove HelioBank's responsibility for deployment, customer communication, threshold decisions, monitoring and complaint handling in its own loan process. Once PreScore Credit Navigator makes that issue visible, the audit stops floating above the organization. It asks whether HelioBank's governance reaches this use, this decision and this evidence trail.

Jonas maps the roles before asking for more files. In the route, the auditor has to understand loan product owner; vendor manager; data protection lead; branch advisor; complaints officer. The point is not to make the chart impressive; it is to know who can explain, approve, challenge or correct the AI-related decision.

The evidence objects are equally practical: responsibility matrix; supplier contract schedule; threshold approval minute; customer journey map; complaint file. For PreScore Credit Navigator, none of these records is magic by itself. Their value appears when they fit together and explain what happened in the sample.

In HelioBank, comfort and evidence pull in different directions. The comfortable answer lowers the pressure in the room. Evidence for PreScore Credit Navigator restores the useful pressure by asking where the answer operates, who relies on it and which record would reveal a break.

By the end of this case, the teaching point is not that HelioBank is careless. The point is more useful: PreScore Credit Navigator can make a plausible management answer too thin when its real use creates a sharper evidence route.
