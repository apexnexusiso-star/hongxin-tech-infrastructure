Workpaper Coaching Notes

A clean workpaper for Lesson 17 would not copy the textbook table. It would name ShieldScore Fraud Monitor, state the criterion for PayWise Payments, describe one disputed transaction that was blocked after a high fraud score and later reversed, list the evidence reviewed and explain why the evidence was or was not sufficient.

The workpaper should also record the boundary of judgement. For PayWise Payments, Victor Hale can judge whether the AIMS handled the issue shown by the ShieldScore Fraud Monitor sample through defined responsibilities and records. The auditor should not invent technical requirements outside the Lesson 17 criteria.

If the evidence is incomplete, the workpaper should show the next sampling decision. One path might be another record from fraud analyst. Another path might be a demonstration by payments product owner. A third path might be a complaint, log or management review note linked to a reversed fraud decision where the analyst note does not match the system log.

This is how a lesson becomes usable for oral delivery. Learners hear the concept, watch it enter ShieldScore Fraud Monitor, see the evidence trail, and practice turning a weak phrase into a professional audit statement.

A final coaching prompt helps close the loop: ask what PayWise Payments would need to change tomorrow if Victor Hale's concern proved valid. The answer should point to ownership, records and review, not only to a nicer paragraph in the AIMS manual.
