Workpaper Coaching Notes

A clean workpaper for Lesson 10 would not copy the textbook table. It would name CivicAnswer AI Desk, state the criterion for CityHelp Authority, describe one benefits inquiry where the AI assistant suggested an outdated eligibility explanation, list the evidence reviewed and explain why the evidence was or was not sufficient.

The workpaper should also record the boundary of judgement. For CityHelp Authority, Leah Morgan can judge whether the AIMS handled the issue shown by the CivicAnswer AI Desk sample through defined responsibilities and records. The auditor should not invent technical requirements outside the Lesson 10 criteria.

If the evidence is incomplete, the workpaper should show the next sampling decision. One path might be another record from service center manager. Another path might be a demonstration by policy content owner. A third path might be a complaint, log or management review note linked to a citizen complaint that the AI-assisted answer caused a missed application deadline.

This is how a lesson becomes usable for oral delivery. Learners hear the concept, watch it enter CivicAnswer AI Desk, see the evidence trail, and practice turning a weak phrase into a professional audit statement.

A final coaching prompt helps close the loop: ask what CityHelp Authority would need to change tomorrow if Leah Morgan's concern proved valid. The answer should point to ownership, records and review, not only to a nicer paragraph in the AIMS manual.
