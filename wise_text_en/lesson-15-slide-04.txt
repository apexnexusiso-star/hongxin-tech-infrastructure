Audit Forward And Audit Backward

Now we can use audit forward and audit backward without turning them into a template. Audit forward starts from the SoA control marked implemented. For the NexaOffice use-control route, Amira follows the SoA to the use rules, then to training, then to permissions, then to the sampled project, then to review evidence, monitoring, and management information. The forward route asks whether the control selection actually reached the place where AI was used.

Audit backward starts from Project Mariner. A team used NexaOffice in a client memo. Amira traces backward to the rule that should control the use, the training that should make the rule understandable, the system setting that should support the rule, the record that should prove review, and the monitoring that should tell management whether similar use is happening elsewhere. The backward route asks whether a real sample can be explained by the management system.

This is where maintenance and effectiveness separate. LumenBridge maintains a SoA, a policy page, and screenshots. That is not meaningless. It may support readiness and design. But effectiveness asks a different question: did those records change how consultants used NexaOffice in Project Mariner, how managers reviewed output, and how the organization learned from use? If the answer is unclear, the selected control may not yet be operating effectively.

Interview Questions That Follow Evidence

Good Annex A interviews are role-specific. Amira does not ask everyone, are the controls implemented? She asks the compliance manager how the SoA rationale was updated when NexaOffice moved from pilot to broad use. She asks the project manager how AI-assisted content is identified before a client deliverable is approved. She asks a consultant for a recent example of a prompt they decided not to use because of confidentiality. She asks IT what can be monitored if content logging is disabled. She asks procurement how supplier terms and model feature changes are reviewed.

The answers do not need to be identical, but they should fit together. In this case they do not. Compliance speaks in policy terms. Users speak in informal judgement. IT speaks in access-control terms. Procurement speaks about the original contract, but not about changes to product features. The gap is not that people use different words. The gap is that the management system has not yet converted a selected Annex A control into a shared operating method.

This interview pattern also prevents over-sampling in the wrong direction. If Amira asks only more compliance questions, she may get more polished policy answers. If she asks only more technical questions, she may get more console screenshots. The useful sample grows sideways across roles, not only downward inside one department. One project sample may require compliance, user, manager, IT, procurement, and sometimes supplier evidence before the auditor can say whether the selected control is operating.
