The Risk-Impact Split Screen

Let us begin with the first split. Traditional information security risk often centers on confidentiality, integrity and availability. That remains important for AI systems. An AI system can leak data, be attacked, lose service availability, or be misused by people with too much access. However, AI risk does not stop there. It also includes biased outputs, performance drift when real use differs from training data, opaque recommendations that people cannot challenge, generated content that sounds confident but is wrong, supplier model changes, prompt attacks, data poisoning and users applying a system outside its intended use.

So, when an organization presents an AI risk register that looks like an ordinary IT risk register with a few AI words added, the auditor should slow down. The question is not whether the table has many rows. The question is whether the method is suitable for AI. Does it consider data, model behavior, human use, affected parties, supplier dependence and the operating context? Can each risk be connected to one AI system and one set of controls? If the answer is no, the document may be maintained, but the system may not be effective.

Now look at the right side of the split screen. AI system impact assessment starts from a specific system and a specific use. It asks who may be affected by system outputs and decisions. That may include users, customers, employees, patients, students, citizens, business partners or other external interested parties. The effect may be direct, such as an automated rejection. It may be indirect, such as a recommendation tool quietly reducing the visibility of a category of people. It may affect one person, or it may affect a group in a systematic way.
